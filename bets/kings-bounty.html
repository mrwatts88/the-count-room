beatable♦difficult count

The published +9 count beats the bet, though the 1000:1 result supplies 59% of the standard scenario's net dollars and usually stays out of sight for years.

The card is already known and close to the ceiling. Count ace through nine as +1, ordinary ten-value cards as −2, and the king of spades as −6. At the standard cut, betting at raw true count +9 produces a 9.97% edge on 2.69% of live rounds.

With a $100 side bet and a flat $15 blackjack hand, the standard scenario makes $17.17 per hundred rounds. That works out to $34.35 an hour at 200 rounds per hour, with a $126,400 bankroll and an N0 of about 491,500 rounds.

That hourly needs a large asterisk. The 1000:1 event lands about once per 992,000 blackjack rounds under this betting schedule. A path with no top-rung hit averages $14.19 an hour. The lower awards still leave the play positive, but the long-run rate is heavily loaded into a result most counters will never see.

1The bet

Any two-card 20 can win, from 4:1 for an ordinary unsuited 20 to 1000:1 for two kings of spades against a dealer blackjack.

You post King's Bounty with the blackjack wager. Only the player's first two cards count. A winning hand receives the highest award it qualifies for, and every other hand loses the side stake.

The official rules include suited ace-nine as a suited 20. They allow six decks or a continuous shuffler, require a main wager, and permit the side wager to exceed the main wager. This page prices the six-deck shoe version.

3The ceiling

A machine reading the full remaining composition can earn 0.3033 units per hundred positions at the standard cut and 0.5372 at the 260-card cut.

I priced every sampled deal position from all 52 card-type counts. At 0.75 penetration, the exact composition is positive 3.19% of the time at an average 9.50% edge. One half-deck deeper, the positive window grows to 4.44% at 12.10%.

The average edge off the top stays fixed at every depth. A uniformly chosen undealt shoe gives the same four-card expectation as the fresh shoe. Deeper dealing spreads the possible compositions farther apart, which creates more positive tails for the count to find.

4Where the value hides

The +9 region wins because small cards have left the shoe and ten-value cards, especially kings of spades, remain.

I walked every deal position through 4,000 six-deck shoes. Even-numbered shoes chose thresholds and odd-numbered shoes scored them. Each position stored the exact side-bet expectation, its second moment, the published count, an exact removal-effect score, the number of kings of spades left, and aggregate ace, nine, ten and king counts.

The count curve crosses into profit at +7. The +9 trigger gives up some frequency for a stronger conditional edge and reaches the best return in the held-out rounded-count curve.

5Fitting a count

The published card takes 93.8% of the standard-cut ceiling. Exact weights plus a king-of-spades side count raise that to 97.9%.

The published card tags ace through nine +1, ordinary ten-value cards −2, and the king of spades −6. Fixed +9 returns 0.2845 units per hundred positions against the 0.3033 exact ceiling at 0.75 penetration. At the 260-card cut it returns 0.5124 against 0.5372, or 95.4% capture.

I fitted continuous weights from the exact fresh-shoe removal effects and added the number of kings of spades left as a second signal. That system captures 97.9% at the standard cut and 97.4% at the deeper cut. Exact ace, nine, ten and king aggregates with the same side count reach 99.3% at the standard cut.

Higher-order terms were considered because matching suits and duplicate kings create interactions. The richer systems leave only a few hundredths of a unit per hundred beyond the published card, so the extra bookkeeping has little to earn.

♠How to play it

Keep one running count over every exposed card. Divide by decks remaining without rounding the result, and put out the King's Bounty maximum when the raw true count reaches +9. The blackjack wager stays flat and follows basic strategy.

6Playing it out of sample

Twenty thousand fresh shoes keep raw +9 positive at both cuts, with 0.2677 units per hundred live rounds at the standard depth.

The live engine dealt one basic-strategy hand through 20,000 fresh shoes. It evaluated every qualifying round from the exact remaining composition, so the certification does not depend on how many 1000:1 events happened to land.

At 0.75 penetration, raw +9 bets 2.685% ± 0.052% of rounds at a 9.971% ± 0.157% edge. The result is 0.2677 ± 0.0075 units per hundred, or 88.3% of the full-composition position ceiling. At the 260-card cut, it earns 0.4978 ± 0.0117, or 92.7% of that ceiling.

Actual round starts run a little below the rounded position scan because the live rule uses the unrounded true count and blackjack hands consume cards in uneven blocks. Both live samples use fresh seeds outside the fitting set.

7The ledger

The standard-cut scenario makes $34 an hour in the long run and $14 an hour on paths where the 1000:1 result has not arrived.

The scenario charges a flat $15 blackjack hand its 0.6397% house edge, or $9.60 per hundred rounds. The $100 King's Bounty wagers return $26.77, leaving $17.17. Measured main-side covariance lifts the bankroll by 0.3% against a zero-covariance check.

The top event supplies 0.1008 units of the side bet's 0.2677 units per hundred rounds. That is 37.7% of side profit and 58.7% of the dollars left after the main-game loss. Its extra 900 units above the ordinary 100:1 king-of-spades award supply 33.9% of the edge and 70.6% of variance.

Under this schedule the 1000:1 result appears once per 992,053 blackjack rounds. At 200 rounds per hour, the mean wait is 4,960 hours and the median is 3,438. Reaching a 95% chance takes 14,860 hours. The chance of seeing one by the full-game N0 is 39.0%.

Conditioning on no top event drops the expected result to $7.09 per hundred rounds, or $14.19 an hour. The full expectation remains the right long-run figure. The no-top row describes the experience on the more common path before the jackpot shows up.

8The call

Beatable: the published count finds a real edge, with a large bankroll and a jackpot-loaded hourly.

The published +9 card captures 93.8% of the standard-cut composition ceiling and survives live round starts at a 9.97% edge on the rounds it bets. A richer count has too little headroom to justify carrying it.

The standard scenario earns $17.17 per hundred rounds on a $126,400 bankroll. Its long-run hourly is $34.35 at 200 rounds per hour. A no-jackpot path averages $14.19, and the top result takes 4,960 hours on average to arrive. The label is beatable, with the long payout tail doing much of the work.

§The Pennsylvania 1000-200-50-25-9-6-4 schedule

Larger king-pair awards cut the six-deck house edge to 16.51% and move the best published-card trigger to +6.

Pennsylvania's standing schedule keeps the 1000:1 top award and raises the next three king and suited-pair awards to 200, 50, and 25. Exact six-deck enumeration gives a 16.507469% house edge.

The same published card works with a lower trigger. Fixed +6 returns 0.8610 units per hundred positions at 0.75 penetration and 1.2639 at the 260-card cut. These are held-out position results. I did not attach a live-round bankroll scenario to the alternate schedule.

§What this verdict depends on

  • The primary analysis is six decks and the BCLC 4/6/9/20/30/100/1000 ladder. A continuous shuffler removes the countable shoe.
  • A $100 King's Bounty maximum with a $15 flat blackjack minimum. The scenario requires the side wager to exceed the main wager.
  • The main wager is six-deck 3:2 H17 blackjack with double after split, no surrender and no resplit aces. It is priced at a 0.6397% house edge and follows basic strategy.
  • The scenario uses 0.75 penetration. The deeper sensitivity row uses the article's 260-card cut, or 0.833 penetration.
  • The live trigger is an unrounded true count of +9 with exact cards remaining as the divisor. Deck-estimation error was not priced.
  • Bankroll includes measured main-side covariance and treats rounds as independent. Long sessions within one shoe can run wider than this planning figure.
  • All exposed cards are counted without error, including the king of spades as a separate card type.
  • The published live firing rate did not reproduce. The page uses the audited trigger frequency and expected settlement.
  • The Pennsylvania section is a separate pay-table analysis and does not inherit the primary ledger.
