The short busts and the long busts want different shoes, yet one balanced count takes 97% of the exact ceiling. At a $100 side maximum it also pays the rent on a flat $15 blackjack hand.

The awkward part is built into the pay table. A three-card bust likes a shoe that breaks the dealer quickly. A seven-card bust needs enough small cards to keep the hand alive before the high card arrives. Those pieces point in opposite directions, and the long awards are large enough that I expected one count to lose plenty between them.

The expected two-count problem never arrives. The fitted card takes 97.0% of the composition-exact ceiling at the site's standard cut and beats the published practical card by 5.60% on paired live shoes. The card is balanced and the rule is plain: convert to a true count by quarter decks and put out the side maximum at +5.

The priced example keeps a $15 blackjack bet flat on basic strategy and puts $100 on Buster only at the trigger. At 0.75 penetration it clears $42.46 per hundred rounds after the blackjack loss, with a $40,100 bankroll at 5% risk of ruin. A one-deck cut lifts the net to $61.55 and lowers the bankroll to about $33,300.

1The bet

A dealer bust pays more for every extra card, from 1:1 at three cards to 200:1 at eight or more.

You post Buster before the deal. A dealer bust wins according to the number of cards in the completed dealer hand. Every standing hand and dealer blackjack loses. When a Buster wager is alive, the dealer finishes the hand even after the player's blackjack hand has already busted or received a natural.

This page prices the six-rung ladder Jacobson analysed in California card rooms: 1, 3, 6, 30, 100, and 200 to one for busts with three through eight or more cards. The name matters because Bust It and Dealer Bust 21 use different events.

3The ceiling

Reading all ten remaining rank counts is worth 0.533 units per hundred positions at the standard cut.

At each sampled position I removed the cards already seen and ran the dealer recursion without replacement. A machine that bets every positive composition takes 12.72% of positions at an average edge of 4.19%, worth 0.5325 units per hundred.

One deck deeper, that ceiling rises to 0.7299. Depth alone never tells the player to bet, since the average composition at every position is still a random slice of the original shoe. The value lives in which ranks remain.

4Where the value hides

Three-card bust value points against the longer rungs, but the four- and five-card awards carry most of the return.

I sampled every fourth position through 1,000 six-deck shoes and priced each one exactly. Half the shoes fitted the trigger and the other half scored it. I recorded the full ten-rank composition, the published count, the fitted count, hi-lo, and depth.

The conflict in the pay table is real. The three-card contribution's removal effects correlate −0.860 with the four- and five-card contribution, and −0.928 with six cards or more. Four- and five-card busts supply 52.6% of the gross return, though, while the long tail supplies another 11.1%. Together they pull the full payoff toward one dominant count direction.

What each part of the ladder contributes

The common three-card win supplies 36.3% of gross return. Four- and five-card busts supply 52.6%, and busts of six cards or more supply 11.1%. The two longer blocks have removal-effect correlation +0.897.

5Fitting a count

Rounding the exact removal effects to one balanced card raises capture from 93.1% to 97.0% at the standard cut.

Jacobson's practical card is −2,−3,−2,0,0,0,+1,+1,+1,+1. Its weighted correlation with the fresh removal effects is 0.9770. Direct integer rounding, with the full shoe forced to sum to zero, gives −2,−4,−2,0,+1,0,+1,+1,+1,+1 and correlation 0.9894.

The fitted card takes 97.0% of the exact ceiling at 0.75 penetration and 97.1% at the one-deck cut. The published card takes 93.1% and 93.6%. Higher-order terms were considered because the pay table mixes short and long dealer hands. A full ten-count nonlinear oracle leaves only 3% beyond the fitted card, so an interaction system has too little room to pay for its extra work.

♠How to play it

Keep one balanced running count over every exposed card. Estimate the undealt shoe to the nearest quarter deck, divide the running count by that estimate, and bet the Buster maximum when the raw true count reaches +5. The blackjack hand stays at $15 and follows basic strategy throughout.

6Playing it out of sample

Twenty thousand fresh shoes keep the fitted count positive under actual round starts, player decisions, and dealer completion.

The live engine dealt a basic-strategy player, including splits and doubles, and finished the dealer whenever any Buster wager was active. Every triggered hand was priced after player play by integrating the dealer's future draws exactly, which removes the noise of waiting for a 200:1 result to appear.

At 0.75 penetration, the fitted +5 card bets 9.657% of live rounds at +5.391%, worth 0.5206 ± 0.0276 units per hundred before the main bet. At the one-deck cut it earns 0.7115 ± 0.0289. On paired all-round-completion shoes it beats Jacobson's practical card by 0.0360 ± 0.0160 units per hundred, a 5.60% lift.

7The ledger

A flat $15 blackjack hand leaves +$42.46 per hundred rounds at the standard cut. The 200:1 tail still asks for a $40,100 bankroll.

The scenario charges the main game's −0.64% expectation on a flat $15 bet, or $9.60 per hundred rounds. The $100 Buster wager returns $52.06, leaving $42.46. I combined the exact Buster second moment with the banked six-million-round variance of flat basic-strategy blackjack. Main and side covariance is set to zero, the same planning convention used on pages where a paired joint ledger is unavailable.

The result beats the game at a $15 table, though the top rung keeps the bankroll near $40,100. A one-deck cut raises the income and lowers the bankroll because the count sees more extreme shoes before the shuffle.

Fees and smaller side maximums

This scenario assumes the $15 main wager is the only price of the seat and that the side bet carries no flat fee. A collection fee applies every round, while a Buster fee applies on 9.66% of rounds. Either one comes straight off the $42.46 shown above. If another player keeps Buster active on every round, the conservative all-round-completion row is +$35.92 on a $47,700 bankroll.

Income from Buster scales with the side stake while the flat main loss does not. At the standard cut, the $15 main bet needs a side maximum above about $18.50 before the side edge covers its expected loss.

8The call

Beatable: one count captures nearly all the composition value, and a $100 side maximum clears the cost of a flat $15 main bet.

The mixed pay table looked like a two-count problem. One balanced card leaves 3% of the ceiling behind and earns 5.60% more than the published practical card on paired live shoes.

The standard-cut scenario makes +$42.46 per hundred rounds on a $40,100 bankroll. The label is beatable. The operating conditions are a six-deck H17 game with this exact ladder, quarter-deck true-count conversion, a $100 side maximum, and no fee large enough to eat the margin.

§What this verdict depends on

  • Six decks, dealer hits soft 17, and the 1/3/6/30/100/200 ladder. Small changes to the top awards change the count and the return.
  • A $100 Buster maximum with a $15 flat blackjack minimum. The scenario assumes the side wager may exceed the main wager.
  • No collection fee and no flat Buster fee. California approvals let each cardroom set both fees, so a posted schedule must be subtracted from the scenario.
  • The scenario uses the solo-counter convention: the dealer completes an otherwise finished hand only when this player has Buster out. Another Buster bettor changes later round starts, and the all-round-completion result is shown in the ledger notes.
  • Quarter-deck estimation and a raw true-count trigger at +5. Rounding the true count to the nearest integer changes the betting window.
  • Bankroll includes flat-main and side-bet variance with their covariance set to zero. It treats rounds as independent, so long shoe sessions can run wider than the planning figure.
  • All counts are kept without error. The deuce is tagged −4, which makes this a harder card than a level-one blackjack count.
  • The published high-ladder simulation did not reproduce. The page uses the exact live audit for its ledger.
